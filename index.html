    What you’re seeing

    The image is NASA Scientific Visualization Studio visualization 5313, the Earth Information Center version of “Change in Night Lights between 2012 and 2023”. It compares yearly night-light composites from NASA’s Black Marble product (Suomi NPP VIIRS). NASA coloured the change: purple where there is more light in 2023 than in 2012, orange where there is less, white where it is about the same.

    TerraJuke reads every map pixel in your browser, finds the lit ones, and places each on NASA’s own legend bar, read from the same image. Cells of 32 × 32 pixels (a 32 × 9 grid) are what you explore and hear.

    How the sound is made
    Fixed rules; the same colour always sounds the same
    What the image showsWhat you hear

    A pixel counts as lit when it is bright (luma ≥ 0.30) or, for dimmer dots, when it stands at least 0.04 luma above its local 7 × 7 base map and is not neutral grey. Its legend position runs from −1 (orange end) through 0 (white) to +1 (purple end), matched by lightness-normalised OKLab colour. A cell is “more” when its luma-weighted mean position is above +0.15 and “less” below −0.15. All sound is synthesized live with the Web Audio API; there are no recordings.

    Each sound is shown as a pulsing ring (a still outline if you prefer reduced motion), written in the caption, and, if you opt in on a supported Android browser, felt as a vibration: more = short-short, less = one long, steady = one tap.

    Limits
    • TerraJuke reads rendered colours, not radiance. It reports the direction of change NASA drew (more, less or little change) and how far a colour sits along NASA’s legend, never an exact amount of light.
    • It does not measure urbanization, population, economic activity or energy use.
    • “Lit share” is a share of map pixels in a cell. The map is equirectangular, so pixels near the poles cover far less ground than at the equator: this is not a share of area.
    • The wide EIC layout repeats some longitudes, so a few places appear twice on the map.
    • The insets are magnified duplicates of small map boxes. They are never counted as extra observations; Tour plays each inset’s box on the map.
    • The legend, insets, inset labels, box outlines and connector wedges are masked out of the analysis. The mask coordinates are listed in the project’s LIMITATIONS.md.
    • Very dim lights, especially near-white ones, can fall below the lit threshold at this 1024-pixel resolution.
